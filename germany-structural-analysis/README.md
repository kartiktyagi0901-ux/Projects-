# Germany: Structural Winners, Strugglers & the Financial Sector

**An interactive briefing on which German sectors win or lose under six structural forces, and whether the financial system can fund the transition.**

▶ **Live page:** https://kartiktyagi0901-ux.github.io/portfolio/germany-structural-analysis/

## What's inside

| Tab | Content |
|---|---|
| Driver matrix | 8 sectors × 6 drivers (digitalisation, green transition, demographics, bureaucracy, infrastructure, fiscal policy and rates), scored −2 to +2 and ranked by net score. Click a cell for the reasoning. |
| Winners & strugglers | Firm-level evidence: Siemens Energy, RWE, Rheinmetall, SAP, Fresenius vs. VW, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, BASF, Vonovia, Galeria |
| 5–10 year outlook | Conditional turnaround paths (autos → software-defined mobility, chemicals → green chemistry) and the risks facing today's winners |
| Financial sector | Banking, insurance and reinsurance, asset management, FinTech, and a peer comparison with the UK and US |
| Risk transmission | Four shock chains (CRE, China exposure, equity-culture gap, cyber), each traced from trigger to transmission to mitigation |

## Key conclusions

- **Winners** sit where the structural forces create demand that cannot be offshored: grid infrastructure, defence, automation and healthcare.
- **Strugglers** face several headwinds at once. Automotive and construction are hit by almost every driver simultaneously.
- **Finance:** Germany has stability and risk-absorption capacity (Munich Re, Hannover Re, KfW, the three-pillar banking system) but **lacks capital-markets depth**. That is the binding constraint on funding a €500bn+ transition.
