Winners

Sectors where the structural forces create demand that cannot be offshored or substituted.

Renewable energy & grid infrastructure

Siemens Energy · RWE

The green transition is the strongest source of new demand. The grid has to be physically rebuilt for distributed generation, and that work cannot be offshored. RWE moved from stranded-asset candidate to one of Europe's largest clean-power investors.

Winner

Defence & dual-use technology

Rheinmetall

The 2022 geopolitical break locked in the 2% of GDP NATO target. Order books have expanded and new plants are being built. Production is capital-intensive rather than labour-intensive, so the demographic squeeze matters less here.

Winner

B2B software & industrial automation

SAP · Kuka · Mittelstand integrators

Rising wages and a shrinking workforce strengthen the case for automation every year. SAP benefits from the shift to cloud ERP, and Mittelstand machine builders are adding digital services to their hardware.

Winner

Health & care

Fresenius · FMC · Munich & Rhine-Main biotech

Germany is ageing faster than almost any large economy, so demand volume is close to guaranteed. Pricing power is capped by regulation and state reimbursement.

Winner (capped margins)

Strugglers

Automotive OEMs & Tier-1 suppliers

Volkswagen · BMW · Mercedes-Benz · Continental · ZF

An existential platform shift to EVs while Chinese rivals (BYD, NIO, Xiaomi) build at lower cost with faster software cycles. Supplier product lines are becoming obsolete, which makes the job cuts structural, not cyclical.

Struggler

Chemicals

BASF

The business model relied on cheap Russian pipeline gas, and that ended in 2022. BASF is hedging with its Zhanjiang Verbund site in China while shrinking its German footprint.

Struggler

Construction & residential real estate

Vonovia · Instone

Higher rates broke the financing model, retrofit rules add cost, and permitting is slow. A shrinking pool of skilled trades limits supply even where demand exists. Vonovia sold assets to repair its balance sheet.

Struggler

Physical retail

Galeria Karstadt Kaufhof · vs. Zalando, Amazon

German consumers have moved online faster than their cash-loving reputation suggests. Galeria has gone through repeated insolvencies, and smaller retailers lack the logistics to compete.

Struggler

Could today's losers become tomorrow's winners?

These rankings are not permanent. RWE already shows a structural loser turning around. These are the conditional paths for the next 5–10 years.

Automotive → software-defined mobility?

Condition: the OEMs build software as a core competence instead of outsourcing it, and scale EV platforms profitably. If yes: the brands, engineering depth and premium pricing give them a path back. If no: long-term share loss to Chinese OEMs.

Conditional turnaround

Chemicals → green chemistry?

Condition: abundant, cheap renewable electricity and green hydrogen arrive at scale. If yes: the integrated Verbund model becomes competitive again. Until then, the period of pain is long and deep.

Conditional turnaround

Construction → retrofit & rate relief

Falling rates and simpler permitting would release pent-up housing demand, and energy retrofits become a large order backlog. Labour supply remains the binding constraint.

Cyclical recovery likely

Winners at risk

Renewables: permitting and grid bottlenecks can delay revenue. Defence: exposed to political budget cycles. Health: exposed to reimbursement reform. A tailwind does not guarantee returns if execution and pricing slip.

Watch list

Financial services: fortress or fault line?

With political instability, volatile FX and uncertain rates, how strong is Germany's financial backbone?

Banking

Deutsche Bank · Commerzbank · Sparkassen & cooperatives · KfW

Deutsche Bank is recovering, and Commerzbank is in play as a takeover target. The three-pillar system has no single point of failure, and the Sparkassen lend counter-cyclically. Higher rates gave margins relief but did not fix them.

Mixed

Insurance & reinsurance

Allianz · Munich Re · Hannover Re

A world-class engine for absorbing risk. Volatility raises reinsurance pricing, so the sector profits from it. Germany is a net exporter of financial stability.

Winner

Asset management & market infrastructure

DWS · Deutsche Börse

Strong infrastructure, but a domestic investor base that is underweight equities. Asset management is underpowered relative to the size of the economy.

Mixed

FinTech

Trade Republic · Scalable Capital · N26

Low-cost brokers are bringing young savers into equities and starting to close the Aktienkultur gap. N26 shows how much regulatory scrutiny a fast-growing neobank attracts.

Rising

Germany vs. global peers (qualitative, 1–5)

Verdict. Germany has the right type of financial strength (stability, risk absorption, development banking through KfW) but not yet the capital-market firepower to fund the green transition, rearmament and digitalisation at the same time. The Aktienrente pension reform, the retail-investing boom and EU Capital Markets Union all point in the right direction. The open question is whether they arrive before the financing gap starts to constrain growth.

→
