Bull vs. bear

Both cases argued with numbers.

Bear: a bubble at $135

  • Unprecedented multiples: ~94x sales and ~266x EBITDA, more than Palantir (~79x) despite growing far slower.
  • Decelerating growth: +33% in 2025 slowed to +15% YoY in Q1 2026.
  • One profitable segment: Starlink (~69% of revenue) subsidises Space (−$619m quarterly operating loss) and xAI (−$6.35bn FY25).
  • Fair value far below the offer: Morningstar puts it at $63 (−53%) with a Very High uncertainty rating.
  • Circular AI economics: AI labs' compute commitments look like the sector's vendor-financing loops.
  • Governance: ~85% voting control and a key-person dependency on one founder.

Bull: the banks' numbers can work

  • Starlink compounding: adjusted EBITDA +86% YoY, with subscribers doubling to 9m+.
  • A launch monopoly: 165 Falcon 9 launches in 2025, more than the rest of the world combined.
  • The multiple compresses fast: on MS and GS 2030 revenue it's 5.3x and 3.7x sales, Microsoft-like multiples.
  • Contracted revenue: ~$26bn a year of AI compute backlog, more than the entire 2025 top line.
  • Technical demand: Nasdaq-100 inclusion adds $22–27bn of forced buying on a thin float.
  • Optionality: Starship reusability and orbital data centres are not in the base numbers.

Verdict

The company is exceptional, but the price leaves no margin of safety. At $135 the market pays today for three frontier bets succeeding together: Starship reusability, Starlink saturation and orbital AI infrastructure. Even with a generous 20x exit multiple, the price needs about 46–50% annual revenue growth for five years, three times the latest growth rate. Risk/reward at the offer is skewed to the downside: about −53% to independent fair value against +14% to the optimistic case.
